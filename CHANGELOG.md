# Changelog

## 0.28.0

Motion, three new scenes and ten new worked examples. Any stepped scene can zoom and pan with a step's `camera`, svg shapes move, trace and morph, charts move and sort into races, and code types itself beside its output. A `plot` scene draws numeric axes whose curves reshape, a `map` scene draws countries, places and routes on a real world map, and an `image` scene shows a photo or screenshot. Most storyboards written for 0.27 validate and draw as before; **Upgrading from 0.27** below lists the three changes that can fail one or move its text, and the fixes list four small changes to how some scenes look.

**Upgrading from 0.27.**

- **Ink on an svg element with nothing drawn is an error.** A `draw` that names an empty `<g>`, a gradient or `<stop>`, or a `<use>` drew nothing in 0.27 and passed `validate`. It now fails, naming the element, so the ink is not silently missing. Name a shape, a text or a tspan, or a group with one in it.
- **The space between two `<tspan>`s is kept.** `<tspan>a</tspan> <tspan>b</tspan>` read "ab" in 0.27 and reads "a b" now, so such a line is wider by a space for each gap, and the fit and small-text checks measure it that way.
- **A table step that names its row with the older `row` key lights that row.** In 0.27 a table step with `row` and no `focus` ignored the key and lit what the step changed. The key now works as `focus` does on a table, as it always did on the other scenes that take older focus keys.

**A camera on every stepped scene.** A step's `camera` zooms and pans to what it names, a ref, a list of refs or `{ "on": refs, "zoom": 3 }`, and `"all"` pulls back to the whole scene (ADR 0028).

- **It works on every scene whose steps take refs but the map, and on code.** A map's camera is its `view`, which zooms the geography and keeps labels their size. The view eases there as the step starts and follows its refs when a later step moves them, such as a pointer sliding along an array. Without `zoom` it fits the refs with a margin, enlarged at most 6 times; `zoom` runs from 1 to 40, and the view never shows past the scene or shrinks it. Ink and pen marks move with the content.
- **`validate`** fails on a ref that names nothing, a zoom outside 1 to 40, an empty list, `"all"` in a list, an unknown field and a camera on a flow scene, and warns when a shot leaves a lit ref out of view. On an svg scene the small-text warning counts the zoom in every state a text is shown in, so detail text can be hidden until the camera zooms in on it. A state step that only moves the camera needs no `focus`.
- er, files, layers and timeline scenes now give their boxes to `validate`, so the ink check for text written `at` a point covers them too.

**Motion on svg.** An svg step can `move` elements by `translate`, `rotate` and `scale`, `trace` lines on from their start, timed to words of its `say`, and `morph` a path into the shape of another path; `class="flow"` marches dashes along a line, as a graph edge's do (ADR 0028).

- A move goes outside the element's own transform and tweens function by function, so a needle turns about its pivot; `""` puts it back. A trace shows a hidden line and draws it at about 40 px a frame. A morph turns both paths into cubic curves and splits the shorter until they pair, so a circle of arcs becomes a square of lines; naming a path after itself restores it.
- `validate` fails on a move of the root or with another function, a trace of something with no line or with a `when` the step does not say, a morph to or from anything but a path or between paths with a different number of outlines, and trace or morph on another scene type. A hidden path a morph borrows its shape from is not reported as never drawn.
- Demos: `examples/primitives/motion` proves a² + b² = c² by sliding four triangles, and fills a tank; `examples/primitives/camera` zooms into a cell and follows a search. References: `camera.md` and `motion.md`.
- Storyboards without a camera or motion render as in 0.27, apart from the fixes below.

**A `plot` scene.** Numeric axes with lines, curves, data, points, arrows and shaded areas, whose numbers can be expressions in named parameters that steps change, so a curve reshapes and a point moves (ADR 0029).

- **Axes.** `x` and `y` take `from`, `to`, `label`, `ticks` (a step, a list, or `"pi"` for π/2, π, 3π/2), `log`, `grid`, and a `prefix` or `suffix` such as `"$"`; tick numbers from ten thousand read as `20k`. `equal: true` gives both axes one unit, so a circle is round. Without `y` the scene is a number line.
- **Parts.** `functions` (y in x, or a curve with x and y in t), `series` of `[x, y]` points as a line, dots or both, `points` with a label `side` and `guides` to the axes, `vectors`, and `areas` `under` a line or `between` two. Each has an id, a `label`, a `tone` and `hidden`.
- **Expressions.** Numbers, names, `+ - * / ^`, unary minus, `pi`, `e`, `sin cos tan asin acos atan exp log ln log10 sqrt abs sign floor ceil round mod min max`, and `sum(k, from, to, term)`. They are parsed by `shared/expr.mjs` and evaluated from the tree, never run as code, and read only the scene's `params` and x or t. A label shows a value with `{expr}` or `{expr:0}`.
- **Steps.** `set` changes parameters and every part that reads one moves through the values between; a sum's bound between two whole numbers adds its last term in part, so terms grow in. `trace` draws a line on across the step's words and moves the step's parameters with it, so a marker rides the line. `when` starts a step's changes at a cue word. `show`, `hide`, `tint`, `focus`, `callout` and ink work as on the other stepped scenes; a parameter's name lights the parts that read it.
- **`validate`** fails on an expression that does not parse, at its column, on a name that is no parameter, on a trace of a point and on a `when` the step never says. It draws every step and warns on a line with no value in its range, a part off the plot, labels that overlap each other or the axis text or run off the stage, and tick labels that overlap. The highlight check reads part labels, and `mr-cast steps` lists what each step sets and traces.
- **The demo,** `examples/primitives/plot/`, builds a square wave from 1, 3, 5 and 15 sine waves and then traces $10,000 at 7% against 3% inflation, with every number computed and checked in its `generate.mjs`.

**A `map` scene.** A real world map for where things are and how they move: countries filled as the voice names them, borders drawn on, and routes that grow from origin to destination (ADR 0030).

- **The outline.** Natural Earth 5.1.2's admin-0 countries, public domain, bundled at 1:110m (82 KB) and 1:50m simplified to 0.02° (329 KB) by `scripts/world-data.mjs`. The scene draws 1:50m when a view zooms in or names a country too small for 1:110m, such as Singapore. Nothing is fetched at runtime; `d3-geo` 3.1.1 projects it.
- **What goes on it.** Countries by ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 code or name; `places` at `[lat, lng]` or on a country's label point; `routes` through place ids or points, each leg a great circle; `lines` along a parallel or meridian between two longitudes or latitudes; `regions` from an outline or a group of countries. `projection` is `equal-earth`, `natural-earth` or `mercator`.
- **Steps.** `view` moves the camera to `"world"`, a box, or round some refs, along a smooth zoom that pulls out and back in on long moves. `trace` draws a route or line on from its first stop, arriving when the step's words end or at its `when` words. `show`, `hide`, `tint`, `set` (a label) and `callout` work as on the other stepped scenes, and ink draws on a map like any other.
- **`validate`** fails on an unknown country (suggesting the code), a point out of range (naming a `[lng, lat]` swap), a route stop that is no place, an id named like a country, and a `when` the say lacks. It warns when labels overlap or run off the stage at a step's view, when a step lights something its view leaves off screen, and when a view is narrower than 0.5°. The highlight check reads map scenes, countries by name included.
- `examples/primitives/map` flies Singapore to London on the great circle, then traces the 49th parallel from the Lake of the Woods to the coast and zooms on Point Roberts, with its facts and sources in its `generate.mjs`.

**Charts that move.** A chart step's `set` gives new values, a series by name or one value `"series@label"`, and the bars, points and slices move to them as the step starts while the printed values count. `sort` orders bars and slices by value, on the scene or per step, and bars that change places slide past each other, so `horizontal` bars with a `callout` for the year make a bar chart race. New `kind`s `pie` and `donut` draw one series as slices with a legend of values and shares, and `stacked` stacks bar series with each stack's total above it. Chart scenes take a `callout`. Existing charts render as in 0.27 apart from the legend's unit, which the fixes below drop (ADR 0031).

- **`validate`** fails on a set that names no series or value, a series set with the wrong number of values, a negative bar or slice, a pie or donut with other than one series or with every slice 0 at a step, `horizontal`, `stacked` or `sort` on a line, an `annotation` on a horizontal chart or a pie, and `show` or `hide` on a chart. It warns past 8 slices, and on value labels that run together in any step's values.
- A chart step with no `focus` lights what it sets. `mr-cast steps` lists each step's new values and sorting.

**Code that types itself.** `typing: true` on a code scene types each step's lines in behind a cursor over its `say`; lines no step names are there from the start, and removed lines are not drawn, so a diff hunk shows its new code being written. A step's `output` prints into a terminal-like pane beside the code, at the words of `outputWhen` or once the step is typed, and `outputTitle` names the pane. Existing code scenes render pixel for pixel as in 0.27 (ADR 0031).

**Pictures.** A new `image` scene shows a photo, screenshot or figure, and a compare cell can be `{ "image": ... }` (ADR 0032).

- **Sources.** `src` is a path relative to the run directory or the repository, or an http(s) URL. `validate` copies a local file into the run's `images/` folder by its content hash, or downloads a URL there once by the URL's hash, and writes `images.json`. The player serves only those copies and renders read only them, so nothing loads from the network at play or render time. The validate hook downloads nothing.
- **Checks.** A picture must be a PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF file of up to 10 MB, checked by its first bytes and, for a URL, by the server's content type too. SVG files are refused because they can carry scripts. `validate` fails on a missing file, a file outside the run directory and the repository, a file of the wrong type, an oversized one, and a URL it cannot download, with the reason. It warns when a picture is drawn more than twice its own size.
- **The scene.** `fit` (`contain` or `cover`), a `caption`, a `credit`, `kenBurns` for a slow pan and zoom between two regions or boxes, and `regions` as fractional boxes that steps light, ink draws on, and the highlight check reads by `label`.
- mr-cast does not make or change pictures; it shows the file given, and whoever makes the video owns the right to use it.

New demos: `examples/primitives/chart-motion`, `examples/primitives/code-typing` and `examples/primitives/image`, whose pictures its own `make-pictures.mjs` draws.

**The new parts work together.**

- **One `trace`.** svg, plot and map steps trace the same way: an entry is an id, or `{ "ref": id, "when": words of the say }` on svg and map; a plot's entries are ids, timed by the step's own `when`. On svg the words are where the line starts drawing, and on a map where the route arrives. `validate` reports a trace the same way on each, and on any other scene says which scenes take it.
- **The camera on the new scenes.** Plot, chart (every kind) and image steps take `camera`. A `camera` on a map step is refused with a pointer to `view`, and one on a picture with `kenBurns` is refused, since the picture already moves. Chart boxes now reach `validate`, so it warns when a camera crops a lit bar or slice, and the soft-picture warning counts how close a pan, zoom or camera brings the picture.
- **Ink follows what it marks.** A circle round a bar slides with the bar when a sort moves it, and ink on a plot point, a moved svg shape or a map place moves with it as each later step settles.

**Ten new worked examples, beyond software.** `math/fourier` (a square wave from sine waves), `money/compound-interest` (the rule of 72 against inflation), `science/gps-relativity`, `science/mrna-vaccine`, `tech/docker-100s`, `hardware/cpu-adder`, `geography/49th-parallel`, `product/raspberry-pi` (Pi 5 against Pi 4), `cooking/bread` (baker's percentages) and `language/ser-estar`. Each `generate.mjs` computes and checks its numbers and names its sources. The gallery groups the examples by subject, and the skill lists all twenty-one in `references/examples.md` with the scenes each uses, so SKILL.md stays inside its budget (ADR 0034).

**Bounds from the review** (ADR 0035). Each has a test that fails without it.

- **Picture URLs download from public hosts only.** `validate` resolves the host and refuses a URL when any of its addresses is loopback, private, link-local (such as 169.254.169.254), ULA, `0.0.0.0`, `::` or another reserved block, IPv4-mapped forms included. It connects to the address it checked, follows at most 5 redirects and checks each again. A local path that is a link leading out of the run directory and the repository is refused. A server may send a picture as `application/octet-stream`, `binary/octet-stream` or `image/jpg`, since the first bytes decide its type. `MR_CAST_IMAGES_ALLOW_LOOPBACK=1` is a testing switch for mr-cast's own tests (docs/cli.md).
- **Plot expressions are finite.** Parentheses, minus signs, powers and calls nest at most 200 deep, a parse error at the column. Sums whose bounds are numbers add at most 10,000 terms at a point, nested ones multiplied, or the expression does not parse; every value draws its sums from one budget of 100,000 terms, past which it is empty and `validate` says the line ran out of terms. A 200,000-term `+` chain computes instead of overflowing the stack.
- **A route leg between opposite points of the globe fails** `validate`, naming the stop, since every great circle joins them; a stop between them picks the way.
- **Names such as `constructor`, `toString` and `__proto__`** as a scene type, a projection, a move's function or a palette colour are reported as unknown; `validate` threw on some and accepted others.
- **The world outline loads only for a storyboard with a map.** The player's main chunk is 417 kB smaller (2,408 to 1,992 kB, 770 to 611 kB gzipped); a map fetches the outline as two chunks.

**Fixes the examples found** (ADR 0034). Each has a test that fails without it, and the examples dropped their workarounds.

- **plot.** A part a step traces starts undrawn, as a traced map route does, so it no longer shows during the narration before its trace; `hidden` is no longer needed. Lines are drawn about every 2 screen pixels for the camera's view, so 50 sine waves at zoom 3 stay smooth; labels and ink stay where the plain samples put them.
- **svg.** A `move` given as `{ "to": transform, "when"?: words }` plays evenly from its words to the end of the step's voice, so a full orbit no longer spins in half a second; a plain string tweens as before. A `<tspan id>` has its own box, laid out run by run in the player's font, so ink, the camera and focus can name one word of a sentence, and the space between two tspans is kept. `validate` fails on ink that names an element with nothing on the stage instead of drawing nothing.
- **chart.** `prefix` prints before each value, after any minus sign, so `"$"` reads "$35" rather than "35 $". The legend no longer repeats the unit. A `write` beside a chart label steps out past the bar above it instead of landing in the bar's foot.
- **text.** A question lead keeps the right fifth of the card for its question mark, so `sides` and items never run under it, and the fit check reports any text that would. A lone question's mark is at most 585 px tall.
- **compare and every fitted text.** A written `"\n"` breaks the line and counts toward the slot's lines and height, so a compare row grows to hold it and the fit check reports too many lines.
- **table.** A cell that is only a percentage, such as "100%", is drawn as text, not code.
- **The highlight check.** "a two" in speech is no longer read as `a[2]` for a row named `a`: the spoken "name index" form needs a row name of three or more letters, and short names use "a cell 2" or "a index 2".
- **code.** Dockerfiles are coloured (`language: "docker"` or `"dockerfile"`), from Prism's grammar copied into `src/utils/prismDocker.ts`.
- **timeline.** `unit` also takes `wk`, `mo` and `yr`.
- **What 0.27 storyboards see.** Every scene of every older example was rendered at 0.27 plus the new scenes and again with these fixes: 211 of 227 stills are pixel-identical. The 16 that differ are the four intended changes: ten charts lose the legend's "· unit", three question cards draw their mark a little smaller and further right, one table draws its percentages in the text font, and two plots draw their lines through more points.
- CONTRIBUTING says to copy an example before running `voice`, `play` or `render` on it by hand, since they write `audio/` and `voiceover.json` into the directory.

## 0.27.1

mr-cast can list and stop the servers it left running. Agents start `mr-cast play` and `mr-cast plan` in the background and forget them, so players piled up on ports 4747, 4748 and on, with nothing to say which run each served (ADR 0027).

- **`mr-cast ps`** lists every running mr-cast process of yours, from any directory: players, plan pages, renders, and play's voice and model download children under their player. Each line has the pid, age, the pages open or the time until an idle server exits, the URL with its token, and the run and repository. `--json` prints the same as JSON.
- **`mr-cast stop <pid|run|all>`** stops them, with their children. A run name matches the current repository's processes, and `--any-repo` every repository's. `--idle` keeps only servers with no page open, `--older-than 2h` (or `30m`, `1d`) only older ones, and `--dry-run` prints what it would stop. It sends SIGTERM, then SIGKILL after 5 seconds only to what still runs. A render closes its Chrome when stopped. `stop --choices --json` prints a menu like `prune --choices`, each option's command `stop` with the pids it stops; a pid that has exited by the time it runs is skipped with a note.
- **Only mr-cast processes are stopped.** play, plan, render and the voice children record themselves in `~/.local/state/mr-cast/procs` (`$XDG_STATE_HOME`), a 0700 directory of yours, with the start time and command line `ps` shows. A record counts only while that pid is still yours, started then, and running that command line, so a reused pid is never stopped. A record is removed only when `ps` shows its pid gone or reused; a failed `ps`, or one under another time zone, removes nothing. `stop` checks again just before each signal, and refuses any pid that is not a running mr-cast process of yours.
- **Processes of 0.27.0 and older** recorded nothing. `ps` finds node running `bin/mr-cast.mjs` in the process list, by an absolute or relative path, with its port and directory, and marks it `unregistered`; `stop` stops it by pid or with `all`. A shell or `caffeinate` whose arguments name mr-cast is never taken for one. `MR_CAST_PROCS_SCAN=0` turns this scan off for tests and sandboxes.
- **Idle servers exit.** A player or plan page with no page open exits by itself after 30 minutes, counted from the last page closing, or from the start if none opened. A player does not count while any voicing runs, and `ps` shows when each idle server will exit. `--idle-exit <minutes>` changes it, and `--idle-exit 0` keeps it running.
- **One player per run.** A second `play` of a run stops the player already running for it, and its voicing, then starts with its own flags. A first-run voice model download keeps going, and the new player waits for it. A second `plan` does the same for the plan page. The old one is stopped only once the storyboard or plan validates.
- **The prune skill** offers running processes as a last group, `Running processes`, and stops the picked ones only through `mr-cast stop`. `prune --choices` includes that group. `mr-cast status` prints `running: 2 players and 1 render` when anything runs, and the mr-cast skill says that `ps` lists the servers and `stop` stops them.
- Tests cover the record and its removal, stale and reused pids, a forged record for a process that is not mr-cast, wrapper shells, relative script paths, another time zone and a failed `ps`, the registry directory, the selection and menu, idle exit at the server and from `play` during an edit's voicing, a second `play` replacing the first while a download runs, and, where Chrome is cached, a stopped render's Chrome. Example renders in CI record themselves in a temp state directory.

## 0.27.0

Four changes. mr-cast voices with Kitten TTS by default, the tests scene is a behaviour matrix, a new `text` scene puts words on screen as the picture, and Claude can write and draw on any stepped scene with ink that stays until a step erases it.

**A new default voice.** mr-cast now voices with Kitten TTS, which sounds more natural than Kokoro, and Kokoro stays as an option. A voice is named with its engine, such as `kitten:luna` (the new default) or `kokoro:af_heart`, and `mr-cast voices` lists them all (ADR 0023).

- **Kitten TTS by default.** The voice is `kitten:luna` from KittenML's Kitten TTS mini 0.8 (80M parameters, Apache 2.0). It runs offline on the CPU in Node, like Kokoro, and its first use downloads about 82 MB into `~/.cache/mr-cast/kitten`. Each file is kept only when its sha256 matches the pinned one. Kitten has eight voices: bella, luna, rosie and kiki (female), and jasper, bruno, hugo and leo (male), all American English.
- **Kokoro is still there.** `--voice kokoro:af_heart`, `--engine kokoro`, or a Kokoro name on its own, such as `--voice af_heart` or `--voice bm_george`, voices with Kokoro, so commands written for 0.26 keep their voice. Only the engine in use is downloaded: someone who keeps the default never downloads Kokoro.
- **Choose a model, then a voice.** `--engine kitten|kokoro` picks an engine and its default voice, and `--voice` picks a voice by `engine:name` or by name. The voice chosen last is remembered with its engine beside the pace, `mr-cast status` prints it as `last voice`, and the skill passes it to every command.
- **`mr-cast voices`** lists each engine with its download size and whether it is downloaded, then each voice with its description, marking the default and the last chosen. `mr-cast voices --sample kitten:bruno` voices one sentence into a WAV file to hear before choosing; `--out` names the file, and `--pace` or `--speed` sets its speed. Its first use of an engine downloads that engine's model.
- **Clips are keyed by engine too**, so a Kokoro clip never voices a Kitten scene. Clips voiced in Kokoro before 0.27 are voiced again on the next voice-over.
- **`mr-cast prune`** lists the Kitten model as `cache:kitten` beside the Kokoro model, `cache:models`, and refuses to delete either while a download writes into it.
- **A first download survives edits.** While `play` waits for a model, an edit no longer stops its download and starts it again, and a download stopped part way leaves no partial file behind for good. A download that sends nothing for 120 s gives up. `HF_ENDPOINT` names a Hugging Face mirror to download from.
- Kitten speaks a little more slowly than Kokoro, so a video is about 10% longer at the same pace.

**The tests scene is a behaviour matrix.** Rows are the situations the code meets ("when the receiver answers 200"), columns are the outcomes it promises (Result, Calls, Waits, Body), and each cell holds the promised value, such as "3" or "1, 4, 16 s". It shows which test pins down which value, which promises nothing checks, and which situations no test tries (ADR 0024).

- **The matrix.** `outcomes`, `situations` (`id`, `when`, `expect` by outcome, `gap`) and `tests` (`name`, `status`, `checks` as `"situation.Outcome"`, `hunkId`, `was`), with `when` naming the situations column. A cell a test checks is green with a tick and NEW or CHANGED; a promised cell no test checks is amber and dashed, "unchecked"; an outcome a situation leaves out is a faint dash; a situation with `gap` and no `expect` is a red dashed bar holding what goes wrong. A removed test's `was` shows struck through in the cells it checked.
- **Steps** focus a situation, a cell such as `"retry.Calls"`, an outcome, or a test's name, which lights every cell it checks and shows the name and status in the header callout. A step's own `callout` replaces it.
- **Test names stay on screen.** The tests that check each row are listed in a column at the right when every value still fits on one line beside it; otherwise each row's header counts them. The subject and the counts ("7 tests · 4 new · 2 changed · 1 removed · 2 unchecked · 2 gaps") sit under the matrix.
- **`validate`** fails on a check naming a missing situation or outcome, a duplicate situation id or outcome, and an `expect` key that is no outcome. It warns on a live test checking a cell with no promised value and on `gap` beside `expect`. Every value, situation, gap and name is fitted as the player draws it, and a matrix that does not fit at a readable size fails. The highlight check now reads tests scenes. The warning past 10 test cases is gone.
- **Older storyboards** with `cases` and `gaps` still validate and render: each case reads as a situation named by its test, with one outcome, "Proves", and each gap as a gap row. Steps that named a case or `"gap:0"` light its row. A case's `badge` and `tone`, and the `subject` and `none` titles, are no longer drawn.
- **The coverage map.** `examples/primitives/tests-map/` draws the webhook's decision graph with `tint`, green where a test goes and red where none does, as a pattern the tests reference points at.
- `examples/primitives/tests` uses the matrix, and its gallery still is new. `outline` lists a tests scene's tests, situations and gaps, and `validate`'s summary counts tests and gaps.
- Tests cover each check, the refs, the callout, the names column and the counts, column widths, the coverage of a test's hunk, and the older shape.

**A `text` scene.** A new `text` scene puts words on screen as the picture. It sets one lead, and the field name says how it is drawn: a `headline`, a `question` beside a large question mark, a `number` whose figure counts up, a `term` over a rule and its meaning, or a `quote` after a large quote mark. A `kicker` sits above it, a `note` under it, and up to 6 `items` or two `sides` under that (ADR 0026).

- **Steps rewrite the words.** A step's `set` replaces the lead, `note` or `kicker` from that step on, and the old text fades into the new. A number set to a new number with the same symbols around it counts from the old figure to the new, such as 500 to 720,000. `hidden`, `show` and `hide` bring in items, the note, the kicker, the lead or a side, and a step's pen mark draws on the words it points at.
- **`validate` checks every state.** It fits the text of the first state and of every state a `set` makes, and reports text that does not fit at the step that set it. It fails on two leads, items beside sides, more than 6 items or 4 in a side, and a ref that names nothing; it warns once per hidden ref no step shows, naming the items left out, and when the voice says a count ("three things") the items disagree with.
- **`intent` is now an alias for `text`.** Old storyboards validate and render as before: the summary is the headline, the bullets are the items, and the storyboard's title line is the kicker. `validate` still reports an intent scene by its own field names, such as `bullets[2]` and `summary`. The one visible change is the subtitle in the title line, drawn in the neutral grey rather than the fainter one. `mr-cast schema` lists `text` and no longer `intent`, and the intent scene reference is gone.
- **Examples.** Every example's intent scene is a text scene. YouTube now opens on 500 hours a minute, which a step turns into 720,000 a day, and Bitcoin on its question. `examples/primitives/text/` shows each lead, and the gallery shows its still in place of the intent demo.
- Tests cover each lead's fit, text a step sets, the never-shown warning, the spoken count, the number's count, how a crowded scene shrinks, and an intent scene's messages in its own field names.

**Ink.** Claude can write and draw on a scene the way a teacher works at a whiteboard. Any stepped scene's step takes a `draw` list: circles, boxes, underlines, strikes, crosses, ticks, braces, arrows and handwritten text, each written on as the voice reaches its words and kept on the board until a later step erases it (ADR 0025).

- **`draw` on any step with focus refs, and on code steps.** Each item names one verb: `circle`, `box`, `underline`, `strike`, `cross`, `tick` or `brace` on a ref, a list of refs or an earlier item's `id`; `arrow: [from, to]` between refs, ids or `[x, y]` stage points; `write` with `near` a ref or `at` a point. Options: `note`, `when` (words of the say at which it starts), `tone`, `side` and `id`. On a code step a ref is a line number, an `"a-b"` range, or text on the step's lines.
- **The board persists.** Items stay until a step's `erase`, `true` for everything or a list of ids, applied as the step starts. Strokes draw with a moving pen tip, arrows more slowly, and handwriting reveals left to right at about 2.2 frames a character.
- **Placement keeps clear.** Each item avoids everything the scene shows from its step on, pointers that move later and ink already up included. Text with no clear spot goes to the end of its row, or else to free space with a leader arrow, and a long arrow bends or swings round what lies between its ends. Ink moves with a code, terminal or sequence camera, and code clips it to its viewport.
- **On the new scenes.** A text scene's ink names an item, the lead by its field name, `note`, `kicker` or a side; a tests matrix's names a situation, a cell, an outcome or a test, and goes on the cells it covers.
- **`mark` and `note` are unchanged.** They are the shorthand for one item that clears at the next step; old storyboards and review flags render as in 0.26.
- **`validate`** fails on an unknown ref, a `when` the say does not contain, an `erase` id never drawn, an arrow from an id not yet drawn, an id that is also a ref, and malformed items, and warns when text written `at` a point lands on a graph, table, array, memory, svg, state or tests box, and on markup in ink text. A step that draws ink is no longer reported as moving nothing.
- **`mr-cast still --step k`** renders each scene as step k ends, to see the board mid-scene.
- **Examples.** Dijkstra writes each relaxation's sum along its road, binary search circles each look and braces the half it rules out, and `examples/primitives/ink` is a short demo with a still in [docs/gallery.md](docs/gallery.md). The skill's [ink reference](skill/mr-cast/references/ink.md) and [docs/storyboard.md](docs/storyboard.md) describe it.

## 0.20.0

The skill now tells Claude to write each video the way a good teacher explains the change, and in the order a reviewer asks about it. Two research summaries behind it are in `quality/research/`. They draw on worked examples and concreteness fading, Mayer's multimedia principles, refutation of likely misreadings, Google's code review guide, and studies of what reviewers need (Bacchelli and Bird 2013, Tao et al. 2012, Spadini et al. 2019, Baum et al.). No study tested narrated diff videos, so these rules are carried over from nearby fields.

- **One concrete case runs through the video.** The intent states the problem as one real case with real values. The diagrams, the code and the tests then follow that same case.
- **Problem, then the approach in one sentence, then the parts.** The old behaviour is shown before the new on the same case, for a bug with the failing test or repro as a `terminal`.
- **Each code scene opens with the question it answers**, and its steps answer with the reason. After the case, the narration states the general rule and where it stops, names a likely wrong reading when there is one, says what it skips, and ends on what is proven and what to still check.
- **The audience sets the order.** A reviewer's video follows a reviewer's questions: why, approach, how it works, what else it could break, tests. Code scenes go by importance with related hunks together, not file by file. Someone new gets the code as it is today and the terms before the walk. The team overview gets problem, approach, user impact, risk and test status.

## 0.19.0

Five new scene types, chosen from research on how pictures help people understand. A layout should match the shape of the idea (Tversky, Morrison and Betrancourt 2002). Comparisons work best side by side with the differences marked (Gleicher et al. 2011). Program state is easier to follow as frames, objects and arrows that change step by step (Guo 2013, Python Tutor).

- **`compare`.** Two sides, such as before and after, two designs or two stores, row by row at the same height. A row that differs gets an arrow between the sides, and a row that is the same dims behind an `=`. A `verdict` marks the better side green and the worse red. A cell is text or a short code snippet. Steps focus a row, a side (`"side:After"`) or a cell (`"2@After"`).
- **`terminal`.** A shell session in a window. Each command types out, its output prints line by line, and a non-zero `exit` tints the entry red. Long lines wrap, and a long session scrolls instead of shrinking the text. Steps focus an entry or an output line (`"2:3"`). Use it for repro steps, a migration or a test run.
- **`layers`.** Stacked bands in their real order, with the parts inside each band. Use it for a layered architecture, a middleware chain or C4 levels. A `path` sends a request token down through its stops and, with `return`, back up with a reply. `skip` answers early at one stop, such as a rate limiter returning 429, and fades the layers the request never reached.
- **`array`.** A row of cells with named `pointers` above and shaded `ranges` below. Each step can `set` values, `move` a pointer or range, and `show` or `hide` one. Changes animate before the words. Use it for binary search, sliding windows, cursors, ring buffers and off-by-one bugs.
- **`memory`.** Call frames with their variables, and objects with their fields. A value `{ "ref": "c1" }` draws an arrow to that object, so two arrows into one object show aliasing. Steps can re-point a reference, change a value, or show and hide frames and objects. Names may contain "/", such as a cache keyed by URL path.
- The skill now says to pick the scene whose shape matches the idea, and lists which scene fits which kind of idea.
- A pen circle on a narrow item in a dense row, such as one array cell, now hugs the item instead of covering its neighbours.
- `validate` rejects a state step that lights nothing (one that only hides, or sets nothing, with no `focus`), an empty terminal entry, and a terminal prompt too long to leave room for the command. Messages say "an array scene", not "a array scene".

## 0.10.0

- **Diagrams draw before the voice.** Every scene type has a build time in one table in `shared/timing.mjs`, at most 1.8 seconds, and its narration starts 0.3 seconds after the build. Sequence messages, timeline events, chart bars, state and ER changes, tests and table rows no longer arrive spread over the scene, so the voice never names something that is not on screen yet. Long builds stagger faster instead of waiting longer.
- **Steps on diagrams.** `sequence` (`messages`), `timeline` (`events`), `flow` (a `say` on each step), `state` (`state` or `transition`), `er` (`entity`) and `chart` (`label`) take `steps` with their own spoken words, like code, table and files steps. Sequences and timelines reveal each step's items 0.5 seconds before its words; state, ER and chart scenes draw everything first and light each step's item while the rest dims. `validate` checks every reference, and `outline` counts the spoken steps.

## 0.9.0

- **Choices before the video.** The skills ask who the video is for (a reviewer, someone new to the code, or a quick overview for the team), which areas to go deeper on, and the pace. All three are multiple choice with the recommended answer first, and any your request already answers are skipped. A quick overview is a new mode with diagrams and little code. Before a render, approving the outline takes one click. The player skips that step.
- **Calmer pacing.** A scene builds for a second before the voice starts, and new code is drawn by then. Each step lights 0.5 seconds before its voice names it and holds 1.2 seconds after. Silent scenes read at 130 words a minute instead of 160. Subtitles follow each sentence of the voice instead of drifting through the pauses.
- **Pace and speed.** `--pace slower|normal|faster` on `voice`, `play`, `render`, `validate` and `outline` sets the voice's speed and the length of every pause. `--speed <0.5-2>` sets the speed alone. Both are saved in `voiceover.json`. A new speed voices the narration again.
- **Steps on table and files scenes.** Each row or file can carry its own spoken words and lights just before them. Without steps, the spotlight now follows the narration instead of running ahead of it.
- **Chapter markers in MP4s.** Rendered videos carry one chapter per chapter title.
- **Player speed control.** The browser player plays at 0.75x, 1x, 1.25x or 1.5x.
- `validate` warns when the narration over an `arch` or `sequence` diagram runs past 35 words.
- Fixed: the browser player now reloads when storyboard.json changes.

## 0.8.0

- **Simple mode.** Ask for a walkthrough "line by line", "like I'm new to this", "ELI5" or "for a non-native speaker", or pass `--simple` to `extract`, `explain init` or `/mr-cast-render`. Code is explained one line or small group at a time in short, plain sentences. `validate` checks step size, line coverage and sentence length.

- **New scenes.** `table` (rows with added, removed and changed values), `chart` (bar and line, with before and after badges), `timeline` (events and spans on a time axis), and `files` (the change as a file tree with line counts and notes).
- **Upgrading.** `mr-cast upgrade` installs the latest version and refreshes the skills. Commands print a one-line notice when a newer version is on npm, from a check that runs in the background at most once a day. After `npm i -g`, the next command refreshes the installed skills. Ask Claude to "upgrade mr-cast" through the new `mr-cast-upgrade` skill.
